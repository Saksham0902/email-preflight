Email Preflight

Pre-send checks for Marketing Cloud Next emails

Created by Saksham Mathur  |  Last updated: September 2026

Email Preflight is a panel that lives inside the Marketing Cloud Next email builder. Open it while you are working on an email and it reads what you have open, then lists what looks wrong — a missing subject line, a button with no link, an image with no alt text, columns that will not stack on a phone, and around eighty other things. It also gives whoever is doing QA a place to check the email against the copy deck.

It only reads. The panel can never change your email. The worst it can do is report something that turns out to be fine.

Why it exists

Marketing Cloud Next has no validation step before a send. When something is wrong, you find out afterwards — as a row on the Email Engagement data model object saying "Failed to render due to syntax errors" or "Data graph doesn't contain valid personalization information". By that point the send is spent and the audience already has the broken email.

Most of those failures are visible in the email beforehand. So are the ordinary mistakes that come from building a new email by copying an old one: a link nobody updated, placeholder copy left behind, a subject line still saying "TEST:".

Checking all of that by hand is slow and easy to get wrong — especially when the person doing QA is also the person who built the email.

Opening it

  1. Open an email (or a reusable content block) in the Marketing Cloud Next builder.
  2. Click the extension panel icon and choose Email Preflight.
  3. It scans straight away — there is no button to press.

Click Re-check after you make a change. The panel has two tabs: Email Issues and Email QA.

The Email Issues tab

Everything the tool found, worst first, at three levels:

Level What it means What to do
Error Will break the send, or ship visibly broken Fix before sending
Warning Probably wrong, but there are legitimate reasons it might not be Have a look
Note Worth knowing about. No action implied Read if you have time

The tool never blocks a send. It has no way to.

Reading the results

  • The counts at the top — All / Errors / Warnings / Notes — are also buttons. Click one to show only that level.
  • Where the issues are is a folded list of the email's sections with a count against each. Click a section to see only its issues. This is there because the main list is sorted by severity, which is right for triage and wrong for repair: nobody fixes an email by severity, they open a section and fix everything in it.
  • Each finding says what is wrong, why it matters, and where it is — "Button 'Learn more' in Section 3 of 6" rather than a component ID.
  • The − at the top right of a finding removes it, for the ones you have looked at and decided to live with. A bar appears saying how many you have hidden, with Restore all next to it. Hidden findings also drop out of the exports.

Sharing the results

  • Copy report gives plain text, for a ticket or a Slack message.
  • Copy for Excel gives rows that fill the columns when pasted into a spreadsheet — for a QA tracker where someone marks each item resolved.

Both include everything except what you hid, regardless of which filter you happen to be looking through at the time.

The Email QA tab

For checking an email against what it is supposed to say — the copy deck, the brief, the previous version. Paste in what you expect, and the tool tells you whether the email matches.

What you check What you paste in
Subject lineThe approved subject
PreheaderThe approved preheader
Each link in the emailThe URL that link should point to
Each button or linkThe words that should be printed on it
Each imageThe file it should be
Each imageIts alt text

Links, buttons and images get one box each, labelled by where they sit in the email, rather than one box for a whole list. You answer one question at a time instead of matching a list against a list.

Comparison is exact — but when something does not match, it tells you precisely what differs: a curly quote instead of a straight one, a double space, a merge field present on one side and not the other. Those are the differences that are near impossible to catch by eye and trivial to catch mechanically.

URL comparison ignores tracking parameters, so a link still matches when the spec has a clean URL and the email has a tagged one.

Blank means skipped, not passed. A row you left empty never counts itself as one that matched. Nothing you type is written back to the email, and nothing survives a page reload.

What it checks

Around eighty checks in total. Grouped by area:

Area Examples
Content basics Missing subject line or preheader; "TEST:", "DRAFT:" or "DO NOT SEND" still in the subject; placeholder copy like "Lorem ipsum" or "Your text here"; [First Name] typed as text instead of wired up as a real merge field
Links Buttons with no destination at all; # or example.com placeholders; insecure http:// links; staging or test URLs; a space inside a URL; two query strings accidentally joined together
Images An image component placed but never given a picture; missing alt text; alt text that is just a file name; an image-only email with no live text; a logo that does not link anywhere
Personalization Unclosed {{#if}} or {{#each}}; a {{fallback}} with no backup value; a variable nothing in the email defines; a variable whose capitalisation does not match where it was set; personalization with no data provider attached
Compliance No unsubscribe link; no preference centre link; no postal address in the footer
Layout and rendering Columns that do not add up to the grid; rows that will not stack on a phone; left and right padding that do not match; font sizes small enough that phones auto-scale them; line height tight enough that lines collide; images wider than their column
Mail client support display:flex and other web-only layout; downloaded web fonts Outlook will never load; external stylesheets that get stripped; background images Outlook silently drops; buttons built in a way Outlook renders wrong
Accessibility Text contrast below WCAG AA, with the computed ratio; link text that says nothing ("Click here"); layout tables missing role="presentation"
Dark mode A background colour set with no text colour beside it; pure black and pure white, which dark mode rewrites most aggressively
Size Content approaching Gmail's clip point, which hides everything below the unsubscribe link; base64 images embedded in the email
Migrated MCE content %%FirstName%% substitution strings and AMPscript URL helpers, which MCN does not evaluate and delivers to the recipient as literal text; hardcoded MCE tracking domains
Known MCN limitations Repeaters and product recommendations inside reusable content blocks; dynamic content evaluated against the wrong Data Graph. These fail silently — no error, just wrong output

What it cannot see

Content blocks added to the email

An email stores a pointer to a reusable content block, not a copy of it. So from inside the email, the panel cannot read what is in the block. If your unsubscribe link lives in a shared footer block, the panel will report it missing.

When it finds one, it says so at the top of the results, names the block, and tells you to check it separately. To do that, open the content block on its own and run the panel there. It switches to Content Block Issues and asks what the block is — header, body or footer — so the right checks apply. A footer gets the unsubscribe and postal address checks; a header does not, because asking a header for a postal address would be nonsense.

Templates

Same reason. Anything inherited from a template is a separate item, and needs checking there.

How it actually renders

This is not a rendering test. It reads the content, not the final HTML. It will tell you a font size is too small or that Outlook will not load your web font, but it cannot tell you how the email actually looks in Outlook. Keep sending a preview.

If a finding looks wrong

It happens. Where a check has to guess, it is built to guess towards silence rather than flag something that is fine — a working button reported as broken costs the tool its credibility, and after two of those nobody reads the panel again. But it reads Marketing Cloud Next's internal content structure, and unusual content can be shaped in ways it does not expect.

At the bottom of the Issues tab there is a Content structure section with a Copy structure button. Send that along with the rule ID and what you think it got wrong, and the rule can be corrected against real content rather than a guess.
